# Karibu Learn HC III CPD Curriculum

Status: draft curriculum direction for generated Karibu Learn cases.

This curriculum is designed for nurses, midwives, clinical officers, and other frontline health workers practicing in Uganda HC III-style settings. It is CPD-adjacent: it should support real learning habits now and preserve the metadata needed for possible future CPD accreditation, but it does not currently claim CPD credit.

## CPD Research Anchors

The curriculum reflects the following observed CPD priorities:

- Uganda Nurses and Midwives Council-aligned CPD content should derive from scope of practice, core competencies, safe nursing and midwifery practice, national health priorities, regional needs, and individual learning needs.
- Core competencies include planning and organizing work, up-to-date professional knowledge and attitudes, assessment, resuscitation, communication, patient safety, leadership, ethics, and ICT/digital health.
- Accredited nursing and midwifery CPD offerings in Uganda include pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, postpartum haemorrhage, respectful maternity care, infection prevention and control, partograph use, neonatal resuscitation, emergency and critical care nursing, and leadership/management.
- CPD should help clinicians maintain, develop, update, and increase knowledge, skills, attitudes, competencies, relationships, and professional performance.

Research sources:

- Uganda Nurses and Midwives Council CPD framework references and competency summaries surfaced through Uganda nursing/midwifery guidance documents.
- Aga Khan University School of Nursing and Midwifery Uganda CPD programme list.
- Uganda Health Professional Councils / CPD accreditation guidance.
- Uganda Ministry of Health clinical guidelines and medical corpus already registered in the repository.

## Curriculum Goals

Karibu Learn cases should help learners practice:

- early danger-sign recognition
- safe triage and referral decisions
- maternal and newborn emergency recognition
- child assessment using IMNCI-style reasoning
- infection prevention and patient safety
- respectful communication
- practical HC III documentation
- appropriate medicine and test decisions within facility constraints
- escalation and teamwork
- digital charting habits inherited from Karibu EHR

## Curriculum Tracks

### Track 1: Fever, Malaria, and Acute Illness

Purpose: strengthen danger-sign screening and management/referral decisions in common febrile presentations.

Case targets:

- uncomplicated malaria triage
- severe malaria referral
- febrile child with fast breathing
- fever with rash / measles concern
- sepsis recognition

### Track 2: Child Health and IMNCI Reasoning

Purpose: reinforce structured child assessment, danger signs, dehydration classification, and caregiver counseling.

Case targets:

- cough and fast breathing
- diarrhoea and dehydration
- malnutrition red flags
- newborn fever
- immunization catch-up counseling

### Track 3: Maternal Health and Midwifery Emergencies

Purpose: support high-value CPD priorities for midwives and maternity-facing clinicians.

Case targets:

- pre-eclampsia/eclampsia recognition
- postpartum haemorrhage response
- prolonged labour and partograph interpretation
- breech/shoulder dystocia recognition and escalation
- respectful maternity care under pressure

### Track 4: Neonatal and Emergency Care

Purpose: practice immediate recognition and stabilization steps before referral or escalation.

Case targets:

- neonatal resuscitation scenario
- neonatal sepsis concern
- asthma/acute wheeze
- hypoglycaemia or altered consciousness
- trauma/shock

### Track 5: HIV, TB, STI, and Continuity of Care

Purpose: strengthen longitudinal, stigma-aware, guideline-aligned care.

Case targets:

- TB symptom screening
- missed ART refill
- STI syndromic management and partner management
- adolescent sexual health confidentiality
- HIV/TB integration

### Track 6: Patient Safety, Communication, and Digital Practice

Purpose: make Karibu Learn useful beyond diagnosis by practicing safe nursing/midwifery behavior.

Case targets:

- infection prevention and needle-stick response
- medication allergy documentation
- respectful care and consent
- queue escalation when a stable-looking patient deteriorates
- clear mobile EHR documentation

### Track 7: Outbreak, IPC, Ebola, and Viral Haemorrhagic Fever

Purpose: prepare HC III staff to recognize and safely route suspected outbreak cases during Uganda's current 2026 Bundibugyo virus disease alert context.

Case targets:

- fever with travel/contact history
- funeral contact exposure
- health worker PPE breach
- vomiting at triage
- community fear and risk communication
- unexplained death notification

### Track 8: Emergency and Critical Care at HC III

Purpose: practice first-contact stabilization and referral for unstable patients.

Case targets:

- trauma/shock
- poisoning
- snakebite
- chest pain
- severe dehydration
- airway emergencies

### Track 9: NCD and Mental Health Reasoning

Purpose: make chronic disease and mental health cases practical for frontline clinicians.

Case targets:

- uncontrolled hypertension
- diabetes complications
- epilepsy continuity
- alcohol withdrawal
- psychosis and dignity
- self-harm risk

### Track 10: Mental Health and Psychosocial Care

Purpose: give focused practice for respectful, stigma-aware mental health and psychosocial safety encounters.

Case targets:

- suicide and self-harm screening
- postnatal depression screening
- psychosis with family restraint concerns
- intimate partner violence disclosure
- alcohol withdrawal and agitation
- safe referral and follow-up planning

### Track 11: General HC III Practice

Purpose: cover common first-contact presentations that do not sit neatly inside a vertical program but still require safe triage, documentation, and guideline-aware decisions.

Case targets:

- adult cough with uncertain diagnosis
- dysuria where pregnancy status changes management
- wound and tetanus risk
- common pain or skin presentations
- deciding when a stable patient needs reassessment

### Track 12: Pharmacy, Lab, and Diagnostic Reasoning

Purpose: teach learners to use tests and medicines safely without over-trusting a single result.

Case targets:

- discordant malaria and pneumonia signs
- urine protein in ANC
- antibiotic request without assessment
- low haemoglobin in pregnancy
- stockout-aware referral
- pregnancy test changing management

### Track 13: Day in the Clinic Mixed Practice

Purpose: simulate the real cognitive switching of an HC III shift where each patient is unrelated to the last.

Case targets:

- triage escalation
- delayed lab result
- pharmacy safety question
- follow-up call deterioration
- competing emergencies
- wrong chart risk
- referral refusal

## Progression

Each canonical case can become multiple playable levels.

- Level 1: information-rich, teaches the pattern.
- Level 2: some key history must be asked.
- Level 3: vitals/history/exam are gated and must be requested or entered.
- Level 4: conflicting cues and documentation tasks.
- Level 5: case-conference style, ambiguous or high-stakes.

Recommended Level 1 to Level 3 progression:

- Method A, information gating: Level 1 gives vitals, history, exam, and tests; Level 2 gives vitals/exam but hides one key history item; Level 3 starts with chief complaint only and requires vitals, focused history, and referral decision.
- Method B, EHR task load: Level 1 is recognition only; Level 2 asks the learner to document one focused next step; Level 3 adds simulated vitals entry, focused history, and referral documentation.
- Method C, cognitive noise: Level 1 presents the clean teaching pattern; Level 2 withholds the management-changing clue; Level 3 adds distractors, competing priorities, or a normal-looking early presentation with hidden danger signs.
- Method D, autonomy: Level 1 uses guided questions; Level 2 asks for the next best action after one missing data request; Level 3 lets the learner choose what to ask, examine, document, and escalate.
- Method E, clinic workflow: Level 1 is a single-patient teaching case; Level 2 includes one workflow task such as charting or follow-up; Level 3 places the case inside a realistic HC III queue where missing information must be actively obtained.

Learners unlock harder quests by demonstrating competence: not just by completing cases, but by safely recognizing danger signs, making correct referral decisions, and improving over time.

## Review Expectations

No generated case should move from `generated` to `reviewed` until a clinician reviewer checks:

- clinical correctness
- HC III realism
- midwife/nurse scope and role fit
- patient safety
- referral threshold
- guideline citation quality
- respectful communication
- learning value
